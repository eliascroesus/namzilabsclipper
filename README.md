# Namzilabs clipper

A clipping machine that runs in the browser. Drop in footage and a sound, and it makes finished short-form edits cut to the music, each ending on a demo card for the product it promotes. Nothing to install: the video work runs inside Chrome on the Mac's own video engine and GPU, two small AI models run in the page (one sees what's in the footage, one hears where the singing is), and Google's free Gemini API, if you add a key, sharpens the picks and listens to long videos for Story.

**Open it:** https://eliascroesus.github.io/namzilabsclipper/ (see [Publishing](#publishing) for the one-time switch that turns this on). A second page, **[Mimic](https://eliascroesus.github.io/namzilabsclipper/mimic.html)**, edits raw talking footage the way a reference ad is edited (below).

## What it makes

| Format | You drop | You get |
|---|---|---|
| **Montage** | Clips and photos, and a Reel that uses the sound you want | Your best moments cut on the beat, in the edit styles taken from the reference edits and the designs (below) you tick, each edit in a batch in another: a mood line, a POV caption, a line of text or an animated caption over it |
| **Story** | A long video of someone talking (vlog, podcast, interview), plus a sound | Its best 15 to 60 second moments as clips: the hook on frame one, pauses cut out, word-timed subtitles, a burst of the best shots on the song's drop, then the card (nio.trade's best-performing shape) |
| **Twist** | Flex footage, plus a clip of the other side (the desk, the screen, the work) | "what they see vs... / what they don't...": a montage of the flex, a hard flip on a downbeat to one long shot of the real side (brezscales style) |
| **Meme** | One clip | The clip held 8 to 11 seconds, faded up from black, a small block of text over it, the song carrying it (gillioniare, brezscales) |

**Edit styles** (Montage, under Style). Each is how one of the reference edits is shaped ([measured here](docs/edit-analysis.md#the-styles)):

- **On the beat** (mico, nio.trade, TJR): straight cuts on the music, one repeating pattern from the drop with clips re-cut on the beat, a flash or film burn on the drop. Every other one has TJR's windows once after the drop: over a shot, clips land in the middle of the frame on the beats, each in its own shape and one on top of the other, the shot under them stuttering at 12 frames a second and then freezing when the last lands, and the last takes the whole frame on the cut and plays straight on. The others open up from black, as nio.trade's all do.
- **Talk, then the drop** (TJR): someone in your footage talking, in black and white, their own voice over the song's quiet intro and their pauses cut out, then a hard cut into colour on the drop, and TJR's windows once after it. A sound model in the page tells talking from a song, so a clip's music or singing is never taken for the talking; **Talking clip** (under the edit styles) picks the clip yourself. The song is set so the drop lands a third to two thirds of the way in. With no one talking in the footage, a few calm shots a bar each instead.
- **Black and white to colour** (nio.trade): up from black, the build in black and white, colour from the drop, and a shot or two after it that turn to colour on the beat.
- **Photo burst** (nio.trade): up from black, and early on a shot with someone in it plays on while your photos (or moments of your clips) land on their head on the beats, each cropped to its own face and tilted the other way from the last (the page finds the faces). Then four or five pictures fly in tilted on black, three or four frames each (your photos first, then a moment of each clip), and under them the song stutters, as in nio.trade's …0002: it cuts out and the hit the burst starts on plays again on each picture, rolling on until the next cut brings the song back. Then the edit.
- **Fast re-cuts** (nio.trade): one clip cut again and again a moment further on, over a third of the cuts, speed without burning through footage.
- **Slow and cinematic** (brezscales): a bar a shot, then two beats, slow pushes, no flash.
- **Tick the ones you want** (all are ticked to start): each edit in a batch takes the next ticked style, so posting several a day doesn't mean posting the same edit. Untick the ones you don't want; tick just one and every edit is in it.

**Designs** (Montage, under Style): how an edit looks, on top of how it's cut. Each is one of the short-form edit genres, built from the editors' own tutorials and templates ([the designs](docs/edit-analysis.md#the-designs)): its own colour, what it opens on, what every cut becomes, what the song's hits do inside a shot (a kick shakes or punches in, a clap or snare flashes or flips to the negative), what the drop gets, and how the caption shows. Everything lands on the music, held back before the drop and let go on it, never two big moves within a beat, never more than three flashes a second. The cuts mix their moves (never the same one twice running), as the reference editors do: hard cuts on most plain beats, the bigger moves where a phrase or a section starts, crossfades in runs. Crossfades, pushes and slides show both shots at once.

- **Clean** (nio.trade, mico): hard cuts in warm film colour, a flash, film burn or shake on the drop, a punch-in on the big hits.
- **Crossfade** (nio.trade's own, [taken apart here](docs/frame-by-frame.md)): up from black pushing in, crossfades into the drop a beat apart, nothing on the drop (the hard cut is the moment), then two bars of a picture a beat, each crossfading in over 6 frames and whole on the kick (or, in every other edit, short crossfades with their middle frame on the beat, as nio.trade's …6955), hard cuts after.
- **Reframe** (nio.trade's LARP edits, …5448 and …2531): hard cuts only, the variety all inside the shots. Before the drop the picture changes on every beat or eighth even inside a clip: it jumps closer in one frame (4 to 16%, turned a degree or so) and stays; the first shot on a bar line after the opening comes in black and white, darker, and snaps to colour on the next beat; a crash zoom lands on a beat in the bars before the drop. The drop is a plain cut, then a face lands on the head of whoever is in its shot (the shot freezing under it in some); after it, a step closer now and then and a push in with its quickest frame on the beat.
- **Flash & shake** (a cold edit): drained, dark, blue-steel colour; out of white, a white flash on the claps and on the cuts that land on a hit, the kicks shaking the picture and splitting its colour, a strobe into the drop and a flash on it.
- **Zoom**: zooms in and out on the cuts, through with a smear where a phrase starts, with pushes and spins mixed in, the picture bumping on the beats between; bright, rich colour.
- **Velocity**: speed ramps on every shot, slow motion on the hit and a rush into the next cut, with zoom blurs, shakes and punch-ins on the hits.
- **Cinematic**: letterbox bars sliding in, teal shadows and orange highlights, a glow, light leaks drifting across the opening and the drop, blur-ins on the phrases and a dip to black where a section starts, every clip pushing in slowly, the caption a film title in wide-spaced capitals.
- **Noir**: hard black and white with heavy grain, flickering in, black flickers on the build's big hits, then the kicks punching in and the claps flipping to the negative.
- **Glitch**: torn bands of picture, colour splits and scanlines on the cuts and the hits (every third hit stuttering at 12 frames a second instead), with smears, whips and strobes between; purple and cyan.
- **Whip**: whip pans each way between the shots (out one side, smeared, in from the other), spins, pushes and slides, a spin into the drop, swings on the big hits.
- **Split screen**: the build in stacked panels (three rows in 9:16, two side by side in 4:3), each cut changing one of them, then the drop full frame, landing zoomed in.
- **VHS**: a camcorder tape: soft picture, colour bleeding late, wobbling lines and a torn bottom edge, lifted blacks, PLAY and the date in the corner in VCR lettering.
- **Phonk** (a cold edit, darker): drained near black and white with red in the highlights, the build stuttering at 12 frames a second, then freezing on one frame for the last two beats, then the drop hits out of it (a flash, a hard shake, a glow, a smear); after it every kick shakes and glows, the claps flash white or flip to the negative by turns.
- **Ice** (a cold edit, cool): cold blue, the highlights glowing; crossfades into the drop a beat apart, a freeze on the last beat, then a white flash and a glow on the drop; after it glow pulses on the kicks, pull-outs, zoom-ins, crossfades and slides, never a shake.
- **Tick the ones you want** (all are ticked to start; tick just one and every edit is in it): each edit in a batch in the next ticked design, the ones that suit the song first (a hard, fast song with 808s leads with flash and shake, phonk, reframe and glitch; a middling one with zoom, whip and cinematic; a calm one with cinematic and the tape, and leaves the hard three out), and night footage brings the dark ones forward. Clean comes after the first few, so a batch always opens on something new. A slow edit only takes the calm ones; black and white all through never goes with a style that flips into colour; panels never cover someone talking or a burst of photos.

**Effects** (under Style): leave any group out of every edit: black and white, flashes (with strobes, the negative, film burns and glow pulses), shakes (and punch-ins, crash zooms, swings), glitches, transitions (off: hard cuts only), the VHS tape (its lines, PLAY and the date) and film bars (letterbox, light leaks). A design that's nothing but one of them (the tape, noir) is left out of the batch with it. **At the drop** appears when the stretch of song the edits use has a clear drop (a loudness step like every reference edit's drop has), with its time and a button to hear it: switch off **black and white until the drop** (the black and white to colour style is then left out too) or **a hit on the drop** (its flash, punch-in, strobe, freeze or move into it: a plain cut instead).

**Mark the drop yourself** (Montage, under the song): switch on **Mark the drop myself** and a pin goes on the song where it found the drop. Tap the song where the drop hits, drag the pin by its knob, or press Play and tap **Drop here** as it hits; it lands on the nearest beat (the arrow keys move it a beat), and every edit in the batch drops there. The stretch the edits use moves to keep it in, the drop about two fifths of the way through (a start you picked stays when the drop falls well inside it). Then tag each clip or photo **Before** or **After** the drop in the footage list (**Either** can go on both sides): the shots before the drop come from the clips tagged Before or Either, the drop and everything after it from the ones tagged After or Either, and so do the photo burst's pictures, the pictures on someone's head and the windows' clips. Only a shot that none of its side's clips can fill takes one from the other side. The caption splits in two: **Before the drop**, up to it, and **After the drop**, from it to the end (or the card), each in the caption's look, and with Animated each a word on each beat on its own side of the drop. Leave one empty for no caption on that side.

**The caption** keeps its own look in every edit whatever the design: the **Mood** line in its soft italic serif, the POV label, the Text as you wrote it. Pick **Animated** for each design's own way instead: big tall capitals popping in a word on each beat (Flash & shake, Zoom, Velocity, Whip, Split screen), red and cyan glitch lettering, a film title, VCR lettering. **Colour** (under Style) is the design's own (each shot balanced first), or **As shot**, the design's effects still on.

**Design the caption** (under Style, below the caption's text) to set it yourself, as in CapCut. Start from a ready-made look (Classic, Hype, Box, Serif, Impact, Ink, Invert, Tape, Glow), or set the font (Inter, Montserrat, Poppins, Anton, Bebas Neue, League Gothic, Oswald, Instrument Serif, Playfair Display, VT323), size, weight, colour, outline, shadow or glow (in any colour), a box behind the words, capitals, letter spacing, line width, a turn and opacity. Drag it where it should sit on a live preview over your footage: it snaps to the middle, and a shaded strip shows where Instagram's own buttons and caption sit. Choose how it mixes with the picture: **multiply** sinks dark letters into it like ink, **screen** makes light ones glow, **difference** inverts what's behind them (white over dark, dark over light), and darken, lighten, overlay, soft light, exclusion and colour dodge. It comes on as the design brings its caption on, a word on each beat, popping in, fading in, typed out, or simply there, and the export draws exactly what the preview shows. The look is remembered.

**Open with your own clip** (Montage): tag clips **Open** in the footage list and the edit opens on them, in the order tagged, as they are with their own sound (up to 20 seconds in all), the song quietly under them, or in every other edit with no song under them at all, so the song arrives with the drop (as nio.trade's …6955 does after its stream). Then the edit comes in hard on the song's drop: the song is placed so its strongest drop lands exactly where the opening ends (on the next bar line when the opening is too long for that). **Subtitles on the talking** (under Style) puts word-timed subtitles on what's said in the opening, or in a talking edit's opening, heard on your computer by the same speech model as Mimic (a 670 MB download the first time, then kept), set as nio.trade sets them: a few words a line, each held until the next through a pause of up to a second, a word said again added to the line where it's said ("LARP!", then "LARP! LARP!"), a line said again joined to the one before, and a shouted line in capitals; the caption then waits for the edit to come in.

**The card** comes in with the song muffled behind it, its top cut off as if through a wall, from the card's first frame (as nio.trade's cards do).

**Cutting** (under Style) sets how hard a montage cuts on the music. Every setting cuts only where the song plays something, and on the hits nobody can miss, so a run of stabs in an intro gets a cut on every stab:

- **Steady** (the default): the reference editors' own rhythm, on the song's loudest hits: two beats a shot or more into the drop, their pattern after it, and a cut on the hits that stand out.
- **Hard**: more of the hits, still only hits: every beat into the drop that has one, more of the hits that stand out, and a clip re-cut more often. Where every beat has a hit it cuts every beat; a hat between the beats never gets a cut.
- **Relaxed**: two beats a shot or more, a cut on the biggest hits only.

Slow and cinematic always cuts relaxed and Fast re-cuts always hard; a mix leaves the slow style out when cutting hard, and the fast re-cuts when relaxed.

Without an end card, **Loop the ending** (on by default) ends on the moment the edit opens on, as TJR's does, so the replay has no seam. Captions take **Enter** for a new line.

Every edit ends on **the demo card**: black, a laptop showing your product (or a phone, for an app), the call to action above, the address below, and the hand-drawn arrow between them, slowly pulling out, exactly as the nio.trade Reels do it. Set the lines, the screenshot and the arrow colour once; they're remembered. Or end on **your own video** (a motion design, a logo sting): pick **Your video** under End card and drop it in. It plays whole at the end of every edit (up to 15 seconds of it), as it was made (no grade, its own sound off, the song under it), filling the frame when it's the edit's shape and sitting inside it when not.

Each result comes as an MP4 **with the song** and a copy **without it**, plus a post note. When the sound came from a Reel, the edit starts at that Reel's 0:00, so tapping the sound in Instagram and choosing **Use audio** lines every cut up with the beat. Drag the box on the song's timeline to start somewhere else; the post note then says where to start the sound. Drag the box's right edge to make the edits longer or shorter, from a few seconds to a minute (or set **Length** under Style). The card comes in on a bar line, and the music fades out over its last moment.

## Mimic: copy an ad's edit

The second page (**Mimic** in the header). Drop the ad or video you want yours to look like, your raw footage, and optionally extras (pictures and clips) and music. The page studies the reference on your computer and edits your footage the same way:

- **Captions** in its place, size, colour and style, coming on word by word as your words are said (or however the reference brings them on), from a speech model that runs in the page (25 European languages, Danish included; the words can be fixed by hand or by Gemini, each keeping its time). Press Enter in the words where a caption should go on to a new line, and leave an empty line to start a new caption.
- **Your pictures where you talk about them**: each picture or clip is read as it comes in (its name, the words and amounts printed on it, what it shows) and goes on the word where you talk about it: its name said, however the speech model spelt it ("Imangachi" is Iman Gadzhi), an amount on it said ("5 millioner" is a dashboard's "kr. 5.234.118"), or what it stands for (an agency guru's photo where agencies come up). With a Gemini key it also reads your script and looks at your pictures. They come in the reference's **cards** (its screenshot card for a screenshot, its photo card for a photo, sliding or cutting on and off as its cards do; a list said becomes a run of cards) and **cutaways** (your clips, full frame); pictures nothing is said of fill the reference's own cards. Each shows where it went and why, and can be moved to any sentence; a switch puts them all in the reference's cards at the same point of your talk instead. Pictures don't need downloading: copy one in any tab (right-click, Copy image) and press Ctrl+V (⌘V) on the page, or paste it straight into one of the reference's cards with the card's own paste button.
- **Zooms** stepping in and out as the reference's do, and a jump at every cut in your footage so it reads as a punch-in.
- **Sound effects** made in the page (whoosh, swipe, pop, click, boom, riser, ding, cash register) or your own: a whoosh as each card slides in and out and into each cutaway, a swipe as a card's picture changes, and optionally sounds on the script (a cash register where money is said, or the moments Gemini picks). Each can be heard, dragged along the timeline, changed or taken out.
- **Music and ending**: drop a song (or a video, for its sound). It comes in where the reference's music does (or from the top), as far under the voice, louder or quieter as a whole. Pick its part on a strip of the song (its loudness, bar lines and drops): drag the box to the part you want (it snaps to the bar lines), or jump to the top, a drop or the loudest part, and play the part on its own. On the Sound timeline, drag the music's bar to move where it comes in, or its ends to trim where it starts and stops, as in CapCut. Shape its volume stretch by stretch: drag across a stretch and set its level (or mute it), click points onto the volume line and drag them, or give it a shape at once: down under the talking and back up in the gaps, a fade in, a fade out, a louder ending. Listen to the whole mix from any moment before making it; its black ending.
- **Text designs**: captions as the creators set them now, not one line in one font. Several styles in one video (a base, a smaller one for the small words, a big one for the key word, a script for a marked word, a giant word on its own), stacked a word or a line at a time at their own sizes in a staircase, spread either side of the head, or one giant word filling the frame. Each style has any of 71 free fonts at its weight and width, a colour or a gradient across the word or the line, an outline, a shadow, a glow, a box, and one of twelve blend modes (difference turns white letters into the picture's inverse); words can sit **behind you**, cut out of the frame by a person model in the page and laid back over the words, and captions keep off your face (moved the least way that clears your head, or tucked behind it only at their foot). Words come on as they're said (pop, bounce, stretch up from the baseline, rise, slide, blur in, typing with a caret) or a little ahead, the word being said can light up, grow, sweep with colour or move a box along, and captions can ride the footage's zooms. "As the reference" is the design read off the reference itself: its styles, fonts (matched by drawing each of the library's faces over its words), colours, blends, sizes, layout, places and which captions sit behind the speaker. Or start from 32 ready-made looks: the six inspiration edits' own, measured frame by frame, and the well-known ones (Hormozi, MrBeast, Submagic, Opus Clip, CapCut, karaoke, TikTok's box, editorial, neon, typewriter). Mark a word with `*stars*` for its own style. How each inspiration edit sets its text, and how the page reads a design: [docs/mimic-text.md](docs/mimic-text.md).
- **Captions previewed live**: one of your captions drawn over your footage in the look as it's set, redrawn as you change it. Word spacing and letter spacing have their own sliders (100% word spacing is a normal space); the page starts no tighter than four fifths of one, however tight the reference.
- A switch: **also clip the footage** (cut its pauses down to the reference's), or keep your cut as it is.

What it measured on the example ad, and how each part works: [docs/mimic.md](docs/mimic.md).

## How it picks and frames

- **Picks like an editor, no key needed.** A small picture model in the page (TinyCLIP, 9 MB) looks at stills of your footage and says what every moment shows (a supercar, a jet, a villa, a view, a desk, a talking head) and how much it sells the life. Each edit is built from the best moments of everything you dropped, the way an editor pulls selects: the hook, the drop and the last shot get the most striking of them, and talking heads, title cards and filler stay out while there's anything better. A frame too dark to read on a phone counts for less, whatever it shows, and the first frame, which has to read at a glance, is a bright one when there is one. Variety comes from what the shots show, not which file they came from (eight scenes of one Reel are eight shots; four clips of one car from the same side are one), and two shots of the same thing back to back change the framing, so a cut doesn't read as a glitch. Clips you drop one by one (anything under a minute and a half, and photos) were each picked for the edit, so each gets a turn: none comes back before every one of them has been in, scene by scene, and people, a dinner, a laptop or a hotel room count as the life too (only a talking head or a screen of text stays out, and a clip that's a quarter or more someone talking counts as talking all through, however the picture model reads its other frames; it opens the talking style instead; the filler rule is for long videos). A clip the picture model sees as weak gets its one turn and the second round goes to the flex; one too short for a slot plays a little slower to fill it rather than another clip repeating; the same photo never shows twice in a row. And an edit never hops back and forth: a clip it left a moment ago doesn't come back with one shot in between (A, B, A a second later). When a clip is needed again that soon, it carries on instead, a jump further into it and punched in (a re-cut, the way the reference editors cut one car eight times), or waits. A strip under each clip shows where its flex is.
- **Sharper with Gemini.** With a free Gemini key, Gemini rates the same stills (small numbered contact sheets, one frame every few seconds, never the video) instead. The answers are remembered per file, so the same video is never rated twice.
- **Cut on the beat, the same way every two bars.** Measured cut by cut against their songs' beat grids ([the rhythm](docs/edit-analysis.md#the-rhythm-cut-by-cut-against-each-songs-grid)), the reference editors hold one rhythm from the drop to the end, and so does the app. Before the drop: a cut every two beats on the bar's beats where the song plays something (an intro of pads with nothing on the beat gets a cut on the bar line only), nothing cut in the silence of a break, a cut on the sound it comes back with (a switch sound can land half a beat after the bar), and the shot into the drop pushing in as it holds. From the drop to the card: one two-bar pattern, repeated, on the beats the song itself hits (a beat with nothing on it is passed over, and a syncopated kick gets its cut in every bar it plays), each shot about half a second and none longer than a second and a half, with the best of the flex saved for it. In every bar one clip carries over a beat with a jump cut, and at the end of every four bars (two, in every other edit) one clip is re-cut on the half beat where the song hits it every time (not on a hat), jumping forward in it and punching in: how the reference editors cut fast without burning through footage (a fifth to a third of their cuts). On mico's song the app picks its editor's own pattern, 1, 1, 2, 1 and 3 beats; the planner it replaces, chasing every accent, could hold three seconds, then one, then three. That's steady cutting; cutting hard, it's every beat into the drop that has a hit, more of the hits that stand out and more re-cuts.
- **Every hit you can't miss is a cut.** A hit that rises far above everything within a second of it (a stab out of silence, a snare fill, the hit that opens a section) gets a cut whatever the pace, and so does every one of a run of them. The song's hits are judged against the music around them as well as against the whole song, so after a loud intro a quieter groove's hits still count. A hit much like one a bar or two either side of it is the groove's, which the two-bar pattern already cuts to (counted on its own side of the drop, so an intro's stabs are never taken for the groove's kicks); a hat on its own never counts, and between the beats a note needs the low end under it (a kick, an 808, a whole stab), or it's the tune. On the song of an edit that held two beats a shot over its opening stabs, the planner cut a fifth of the song's strong hits; now it cuts all of them, steady or hard (four in five, relaxed). Against the reference editors' own cuts on their songs ([measured here](docs/edit-analysis.md#the-planner-against-the-editors)), it cut 48% of the songs' strong hits (the editors, 57%); cutting steady it now cuts 63%, cutting hard 78%, and both land on the editors' own cuts more often than the old planner did (on mico's song, steady makes 16 of its editor's 17 cuts and one more).
- **No cut on nothing.** A user's edit, cut hard to a 111 bpm song, cut on the half beats after the drop, where the song has a hat and nothing else, and four times through the near silence before it: 33 shots before the card at 11.6 seconds, 14 of the cuts between beats. The same song now cuts on the bar line of its pad intro, on the switch sound after the song drops out, on the drop, and then on the hit every beat has: 87% of the cuts within a frame and a half of a beat, 93% on a hit, and the one between beats the switch sound itself ([the breakdown](docs/edit-analysis.md#a-users-edit-cut-hard)). Whatever places a cut (the grid, the pattern after the drop, a clip's re-cut, the fast re-cuts' own), it has a hit under it or it goes and the shot plays on; a song is cut in parts, a section that plays another pattern getting its own; and a burst of photos fires from a hit and holds its last picture to the next one. On nio.trade's …0002 sound, stabs out of silence, then the groove: a cut on every stab and nothing between them, where a user's fast cut had re-cut between them twice ([the breakdown](docs/edit-analysis.md#a-users-fast-cut-on-niotrades-0002-sound)).
- **Clean shots from long videos.** A long video is skimmed a frame every second or two, which judges its moments but can miss a fast-cut vlog's own cuts, so a shot could run over one and flash to another scene off the beat. A video from YouTube has a key frame wherever a scene starts, and the skim reads key frames, so when the picture changes from one to the next, the cut is on a key frame in between, known to the frame: shots keep a quarter of a second clear of it, where they used to keep more than a second either side of a guess. Before an edit is made, every frame of the stretches it uses is looked at for the video's own cuts (on thumbnails averaged down from a larger picture, against the change around each frame, so a cut in shaky footage or between two dark shots isn't missed), and the edit is planned again around any it finds, so the only cuts you see are the edit's.
- **Every edit in a batch is different.** Each opens on, drops into and closes on moments no other edit in the batch used anywhere, while the footage has strong ones left (a strong shot seen in another edit still beats filler), and the first edit leaves the later ones hook and drop candidates of their own instead of spending them in its body. The rest of each edit draws on the moments the others haven't shown, least of all the ones another edit is remembered by (its opening, drop and last shot), and each cuts at its own rhythm (one busier, the next calmer). Three edits from a seven minute vlog now repeat 6 of their 47 shots, all in the second and third, and open, drop and close on nine different moments; they repeated 14 of 54, and one shot was the first edit's closer, the second's hook and the third's closer.
- **Face tracking** (on by default, one switch). When wide footage is cropped to 9:16, the crop follows the person in the shot: it holds still while they stay near the middle, glides when they move, and never lets the face slip out of frame. A 230 KB face detector (YuNet) runs in the page for this.
- **Black bars are left out.** A letterboxed film or a phone video inside a YouTube frame is cropped from the picture itself, so no black bars end up in the edit.
- **YouTube videos skip the sponsor read.** If the file name carries the video's ID (as `yt-dlp` names files: `Title [dQw4w9WgXcQ].mp4`), the sponsor reads, intro and outro that SponsorBlock's viewers marked are left out. A long video's first seconds and its end screen are avoided either way.
- **Picks for what the footage is and what the song does.** Footage of luxury (supercars, jets, watches) is picked for its flex, as the reference edits are. Footage of the life (friends on a trip, a party, a day out: under a quarter of it flex) is picked for what's happening in it: someone doing something counts as much as a view, and a party or a jump into the pool is the content, not filler. Either way, where the music drives (after the drop, a hard section) a shot with people doing something wins over empty scenery (a view, a street, a room with nobody in it and nothing moving), and the hook and the drop lean the same way, as long as the people can be made out (not in a dim club); where the music is calm, a view is welcome. The design leans too: the hard ones (flash, zoom, velocity, glitch, whip, split) want movement and people, cinematic and the tape want calm, steady pictures.
- **Graded like the references.** Phone footage comes flat: measured on a user's edit, milky blacks (the darkest twentieth of the picture at 0.12 of white), thin colour (saturation 0.17) and bright (0.52), where the reference edits sit deep in the blacks (0.003 to 0.05), rich (0.26 to 0.36) and darker (0.27 to 0.46). Under a design's colour each shot is balanced on its own first, as a colourist does: its first frame, as it shows, is measured, and its black point goes to black, its white point up, its exposure and colour towards the references' ([the look, measured](docs/edit-analysis.md#the-look-measured)). Then the design's look (the clean one: warm highlights, film contrast, grain and a vignette). **As shot** leaves the footage as it is.
- **Hits on the drop.** The shot into the drop pushes in as it holds; in the clean design the drop lands with a flash and a punch-in, a golden film burn, or a punch-in with a shake, turning over through a batch, and whichever it is, the picture smears out from the middle for a few frames and splits red from blue as it lands. The music's strongest hits after it get a small punch-in. The other designs have their own drops (above). Story clips punch in on every other jump cut so the cuts read as deliberate.
- **Velocity** (a switch, off by default): speed ramps, the car-edit look. Each shot hits in slow motion on the beat and rushes into the next cut, slowest on the drop, and the cuts that open a four-bar phrase get a zoom blur. Footage shot at 60 fps slows down smoothly; 30 fps footage gets a gentler ramp.

## How to use it

1. Open the link in **Chrome on your Mac** (Chrome uses the Mac's hardware to decode and encode, and writes the H.264 MP4 Instagram wants).
2. Pick a format.
3. Drop the footage. Anything Chrome plays works: iPhone MOV and MP4, screen recordings, photos (JPEG, PNG; export HEIC photos as JPEG first).
4. Drop the sound: a Reel that uses the trending sound, or any song. The easy way to get a Reel onto the Mac: screen-record it on your iPhone with the sound on, AirDrop the recording, drop it in. Only its sound is used. (A screen recording rarely starts where the Reel does, so post the version with the song in it rather than adding the sound in the app.)
5. Drag the box on the song's timeline to where the edits should start (it snaps to the bar lines; **Play** plays that stretch), and its right edge to how long they should run. For Story, drag the line to the moment that should hit as the talking ends.
6. Optional: paste a free Gemini key from [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey) (no card) for sharper picks; Story needs one. For Story, **Find the moments**, tick the ones you want and edit their hooks.
7. **Make edits**. Download, post, add the sound in the app if you used the version without it. Delete any edit you don't want with the × on it (it stops one that's still being made).

Your videos never leave the computer. Without a key nothing does: both models run in the page (they download once, about 30 MB, and the browser keeps them). With a Gemini key, Gemini sees small stills of your footage; for Story, the video's sound goes to Gemini and only its transcript comes back.

**YouTube links:** a browser page can't download from YouTube, so for now download the video first and drop the file in. A small Chrome extension that grabs it from inside your own browser is the next step (see the [build plan](docs/build-plan.md)).

## Measuring an edit

```bash
pip install -r tools/requirements.txt
python3 tools/analyze_edit.py reference-edits/*.mp4 --out analysis/
```

The app's own output measures like the references: cuts land on the start of the hit (the best reference montage cuts 3 ms after it, the app within a frame before it and never more than 5 ms after), and every edit is mastered to -14 LUFS with the peaks kept under -1 dB after encoding. Both the app and `analyze_edit.py` measure where each hit starts, in 3 ms steps: an onset detector's beats sit 25 to 55 ms after that, which is how late an edit cut on them lands.

The song's shape is checked the same way. On KETTAMA's Comes and Goes the vocal model finds every sung line of the verse within 30 ms of where a studio-grade separation puts it. From 0:34, where the song drops out at 0:41 and the verse's first line lands at 0:43, an edit cuts every two beats, holds one shot through the silence and the beat after it, and drops on the verse's first bar. After it, where the kicks fall between the beats, it cuts the same two bars over and over (2, 1, 1, 1, 1.5 and 1.5 beats, the cuts between beats on the kicks wherever a bar has them), and in 30 seconds of it nothing after the drop holds longer than two beats.

Most music made on a computer (dance, pop, trap, drill, garage) runs at one exact tempo, so when a song keeps one, the beat grid is measured to a hundredth of a bpm and put on the kick and the snare, not the hi-hats. On a 4:22 garage track at 134 bpm, cuts from a beat tracker alone drifted up to a tenth of a second and, before the drop, 11 to 14 of 20 landed on the hats between beats; now every cut of a rendered edit lands on the beat you hear or on a kick between beats, the same way through the whole song, and a 15 or 30 second clip of it (what a Reel's sound is) finds the same grid, to within 15 ms. Bar lines come from the kick and the bass line rather than the chords, which songs often push ahead of the bar, and a cut goes between beats only on a hit that stands out from what always plays there (a syncopated kick, not the hats). `analyze_edit.py` measures against that grid too.

A song played by hand, or sparse (a voice and finger snaps on 2 and 4), keeps no exact grid, and a beat tracker can settle on a tempo none of its hits keep: the nio.trade Reel sound with the snaps was heard at 117 bpm, so a snap landed on a beat one time in three and the cuts missed them. Now the song's standout hits vote on the tempo, the beats move onto where they start, and a bar line with next to nothing on it pulls a cut less than the snap beside it. The same sound is heard at 87 bpm with 9 of its 10 hardest hits on a beat, and in a 14 second edit of it every snap gets a cut (before, 3 of 7 in the edit that showed it).

A kick that falls every beat and a half (a 3-3-2 groove: trap, afrobeats, a lot of pop) can hold a steady grid at two thirds of the tempo too. nio.trade's lyric montage was heard at 103 bpm, where a third of its hits fall a third of a beat off the grid; its editor's cuts sit on 155. Now, when a grid 3:2 away keeps the song's hardest hits (on its beats or exactly between them) clearly better and the drums keep to it at least as tightly, that's the beat: the song is heard at 155, and the ten other songs tested keep theirs.
